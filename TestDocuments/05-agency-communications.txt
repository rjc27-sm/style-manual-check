Internal Communications Update

Staff are reminded of the following updates from the Secretary for the Department.

Workplace Safety Review

The review found: The current policy does not meet modern standards. An updated approach: Safety protocols must be revised by end of quarter. The committee noted: Incidents have declined, but more work is needed.

N.B. all staff must complete the mandatory training by Friday. The policy applies to all employees, i.e. both permanent and contract staff. Updates will be communicated via email, e.g. the weekly bulletin. The training covers first aid, fire safety, mental health awareness, et al. and is delivered in 4 modules.

The Board has approved the new workplace strategy. The Commission will oversee implementation, and the Tribunal will handle any disputes. The Network confirmed that all regional offices, i.e., the Bureau, the Inspectorate, and the Registry, are prepared for the transition. The Library and the Museum have also updated their procedures, and the University is developing a tailored program.

The Secretary of the Department confirmed that the Government remains committed to improving workplace safety. The Minister for Health will announce additional funding, and the minister has indicated support for the initiative.

Key changes include a man-made ventilation system upgrade and new synthetic materials for workstations. The actress who featured in the training video has been replaced by a professional narrator, and the waitress from the staff cafeteria also participated in the filming. The salesman from the equipment supplier provided a demonstration, and mankind's ongoing commitment to workplace safety was acknowledged.

At this point in time, the agency is not in a position to confirm final costs. By means of a detailed audit, approximately $2.5 million has been allocated. The team will give consideration to all feedback, and during the course of implementation, adjustments may be made for the purpose of improving outcomes. Due to the fact that resources are limited, the agency must determine whether or not to proceed with all components. Staff who are in receipt of the updated guidelines should act upon them promptly.

The deep-seeded issues identified in the review require urgent attention. It is a mute point whether the previous approach was adequate. Staff should not take free reign with the new procedures. The review was intensive purposes only and focused on high-risk areas. Regardless of past practice, irregardless of seniority, all staff must comply. The actress playing the lead role in the training video dove into the performance with great enthusiasm.

The review covered the period 2020–2025. We also examined practices from the mid-2010's era. Results from the 06/11/2024 audit were cross-referenced with earlier findings.
