## Spelling

- **-ize to -ise spelling** (`spelling-ize`): Australian English uses -ise endings (for example, organise, realise, recognise). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling)
- **-yze to -yse spelling** (`spelling-yze`): Australian English uses -yse endings (for example, analyse, paralyse). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling)
- **-or to -our spelling** (`spelling-or`): Australian English uses -our endings (for example, colour, favour, honour). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling)
- **-er to -re spelling** (`spelling-er`): Australian English uses -re endings for some words (for example, centre, metre, theatre). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling)
- **-ense to -ence spelling** (`spelling-ense`): Australian English uses -ence endings for some words (for example, defence, licence as noun). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling)
- **Doubled consonants** (`spelling-doubled`): Australian English doubles the final consonant in some words (for example, travelled, cancelled, labelled). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling)
- **Other spelling differences** (`spelling-other`): Various Australian English spelling conventions. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling)
- **Common misspellings and errors** (`error-common-phrases`): Common phrases that are always incorrect. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling/common-misspellings-and-word-confusion)
- **Judgment spelling** (`error-judgment`): Use 'judgement' in Australian English. Note: legal judgments (court decisions) use 'judgment'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/spelling/common-misspellings-and-word-confusion)
- **Prefix hyphenation** (`spelling-prefix-hyphen`): Use a hyphen when a single-syllable prefix ends with the same vowel that starts the word ('re-enter', 'pre-existing'). The 'co-' family closes up ('coordinate', 'cooperate'). Spellings follow the Macquarie Dictionary. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/hyphens)

## Punctuation

- **Em dash to spaced en dash** (`punct-em-dash`): Use a spaced en dash ( – ) rather than an em dash (—) to set off information in sentences. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/dashes)
- **Unspaced en dashes around an aside** (`punct-en-dash-space`): A pair of en dashes setting off an aside in a sentence should be spaced. An unspaced en dash is correct for a span or a connection between words of equal weight, such as 'author–date' or 'cost–benefit'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/dashes)
- **Hyphen in date range** (`punct-hyphen-date-range`): Use an en dash (–), not a hyphen (-), for date spans in financial years, calendar years, terms of office and lifespans. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/dashes)
- **Hyphen as parenthetical dash** (`punct-hyphen-parenthetical`): Use spaced en dashes ( – ) for parenthetical phrases, not spaced hyphens ( - ). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/dashes)
- **Double space after full stop** (`punct-double-space`): Use a single space after a full stop, not a double space. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/punctuation-and-capitalisation)
- **Spaced forward slash** (`punct-spaced-slash`): Don't add spaces around forward slashes. Write 'and/or', not 'and / or'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/forward-slashes)
- **Double quotation marks** (`punct-double-quotes`): Use single quotation marks, not double. Use double quotation marks only for quotes within quotes. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/quotation-marks)
- **Comma inside closing quotation mark** (`punct-comma-inside-quotes`): Place commas and full stops inside closing quotation marks only when they are part of the quoted material. Place them outside when they are not. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/quotation-marks)
- **Serial comma (Oxford comma)** (`punct-serial-comma`): Restrict use of the serial comma. Use it only when needed for clarity, such as when the last item contains 'and' or when a defining phrase applies only to the final item. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/commas)
- **Ampersand in body text** (`punct-ampersand`): Use 'and' instead of '&' in body text. Ampersands are acceptable in proper names (for example, 'AT&T') and some abbreviations (for example, 'R&D'). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation)
- **Capital letter after colon** (`punct-capital-after-colon`): Use a lowercase letter after a colon (unless the word is a proper noun). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/colons)
- **'and/or'** (`punct-and-or`): Do not use 'and/or' in text. It could mean either 'and' or 'or', which confuses many users. Rewrite the sentence to make the meaning clear - use 'or' alone, or 'either ... or'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/forward-slashes)
- **Ellipsis format and spacing** (`punct-ellipsis`): Use the ellipsis character (…), not a string of full stops. Put a single space before and after each ellipsis. Don't use a full stop, comma or semicolon after an ellipsis (a question mark or exclamation mark is fine). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/ellipses)
- **Multiple exclamation marks** (`punct-exclamation-multiple`): Use only one exclamation mark, not several. Multiple exclamation marks are not suitable for government content. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/exclamation-marks)
- **Nested or unmatched parentheses** (`punct-parentheses-nested`): Don't use sets of parentheses inside each other. Use square brackets for parenthetical information within parentheses, or reword. Also flags parentheses that don't appear to have a matching pair. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/brackets-and-parentheses)
- **Apostrophe in possessive pronoun** (`punct-possessive-pronoun`): Possessive pronouns never take apostrophes. Write 'theirs', 'yours', 'ours', 'hers' and 'its', not 'their's' or 'its''. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/apostrophes)
- **Apostrophe in plural time period** (`punct-plural-time-apostrophe`): Noun phrases about plural time periods don't need apostrophes because they're descriptive, not possessive. Write '6 weeks time' and '3 months wages'. This has been the Style Manual's guidance for many years. Singular forms keep the apostrophe ('a day's work'). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/apostrophes)
- **Comma after 'e.g.' or 'i.e.'** (`punct-eg-ie-comma`): Don't put a comma after 'e.g.' or 'i.e.'. The comma after these forms is an American convention. (Better still, replace them with 'for example' or 'that is'.) [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/commas)
- **Spaced colon in ratio** (`punct-ratio-colon-space`): Write mathematical ratios with an unspaced colon. Write '50:50', not '50 : 50'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/colons)
- **Hanging hyphen** (`punct-hyphen-hanging`): Hanging hyphens ('full- and part-time') can be difficult to follow. Consider repeating the words instead to be clearer ('full-time and part-time'). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/hyphens)
- **'from' or 'between' with a dash** (`punct-dash-from-between`): Never mix 'from' or 'between' with an en dash. Pair 'from' with 'to' and 'between' with 'and'. Write 'from 2017 to 2019', not 'from 2017–2019'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/dashes)

## Dates and time

- **US date format** (`date-us-format`): Write dates as '15 January 2024', not 'January 15, 2024'. Day comes before month in Australian style. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **Ambiguous numeric date** (`date-numeric-ambiguous`): Numeric dates like '12/03/2024' are ambiguous. Write dates in full: '12 March 2024'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **Ordinal number in date** (`date-ordinal-in-date`): Don't use ordinal numbers (1st, 2nd, 3rd) when writing dates. Write '1 May', not '1st May'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **Apostrophe in decade** (`date-decade-apostrophe`): Don't use an apostrophe for decades. Write '1980s', not '1980's'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **12 am or 12 pm** (`time-12-ambiguous`): Use 'noon', 'midday' or 'midnight' instead of '12 am' or '12 pm' to avoid confusion. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **Full stop in time** (`time-full-stop`): Use a colon between hours and minutes, not a full stop. Write '10:30 am', not '10.30 am'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **Redundant am/pm qualifier** (`time-redundant-ampm`): Don't use 'am' or 'pm' with words that duplicate their meaning, such as 'morning' or 'afternoon'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **Comma after day name** (`date-comma-after-day`): Don't use a comma after the day name in dates. Write 'Thursday 31 December', not 'Thursday, 31 December'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **Forward slash in year span** (`date-slash-year-span`): Use an en dash for year spans, not a forward slash. Write '2018–19', not '2018/19'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **Confusing 'bi' time term** (`time-confusing-bi`): Avoid 'bimonthly', 'biweekly' and 'biannual' as they can mean either 'every 2 [time periods]' or 'twice per [time period]'. Be specific instead. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **Time zone before time** (`date-timezone-position`): Write the time zone after the time, not before. For example, '13:45 AEST', not 'AEST 13:45'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **Missing space before 'am' or 'pm'** (`time-ampm-space`): Separate the numerals and 'am' or 'pm' with a space (a non-breaking space in Word). Write '3 pm', not '3pm'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **Capitalised or punctuated 'am'/'pm'** (`time-ampm-case`): Write 'am' and 'pm' in lower case without full stops. Write '10 am', not '10 AM' or '10 a.m.'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **'12 noon' or '12 midnight'** (`time-noon-midnight`): The '12' is redundant. Write 'noon', 'midday' or 'midnight' on their own. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)
- **'am' or 'pm' on 24-hour time** (`time-24hour-ampm`): 24-hour times don't take 'am' or 'pm' - the hour already shows whether it's morning or evening. Write '23:18', not '23:18 pm'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)

## Headings

- **Title case heading** (`heading-title-case`): Use sentence case for headings, not title case. Only capitalise the first word and proper nouns. Note: you may need to adjust the suggested fix if the heading contains proper nouns. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/headings)
- **Heading too long** (`heading-too-long`): Longer headings are more difficult to read and can be confusing. They might also suggest that you have too many ideas in a section. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/headings)
- **Full stop at end of heading** (`heading-full-stop`): Don't use a full stop to end headings. Even if the heading is a sentence, it doesn't need a full stop at the end. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/headings)
- **All caps heading** (`heading-all-caps`): Don't write headings in all capital letters as users could misread words. For example, 'ACT' could be 'act' (the verb) rather than the initialism for the Australian Capital Territory. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/headings)
- **Bold text without heading style** (`heading-bold-not-styled`): Is this a heading? If so, apply a heading style. Heading styles make documents accessible and allow readers to navigate using the headings panel. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/headings)
- **Skipped heading level** (`heading-skipped-level`): Don't skip heading levels - a Heading 3 should not follow a Heading 1 directly. Screen reader users rely on heading levels to understand how content is organised. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/headings)

## Abbreviations

- **Latin abbreviation: e.g.** (`latin-eg`): Use 'for example' instead of 'e.g.' in general content. Latin abbreviations can be unclear to some readers. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases/latin-shortened-forms)
- **Latin abbreviation: i.e.** (`latin-ie`): Use 'that is' instead of 'i.e.' in general content. Latin abbreviations can be unclear to some readers. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases/latin-shortened-forms)
- **Latin abbreviation: etc.** (`latin-etc`): Use 'and so on' instead of 'etc.' in general content. Latin abbreviations can be unclear to some readers. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases/latin-shortened-forms)
- **Latin abbreviation: et al.** (`latin-etal`): Use 'and others' instead of 'et al.' in general content. Latin abbreviations can be unclear to some readers. Note: 'et al.' is acceptable in academic references. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases/latin-shortened-forms)
- **Latin abbreviation: N.B.** (`latin-nb`): Use 'note' instead of 'N.B.' in general content. Latin abbreviations can be unclear to some readers. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases/latin-shortened-forms)
- **Full stop in month abbreviation** (`abbrev-month-full-stop`): Don't put a full stop after abbreviations. Write 'Jan', not 'Jan.' (unless the abbreviation ends a sentence). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases/abbreviations)
- **Full stop in day abbreviation** (`abbrev-day-full-stop`): Don't put a full stop after abbreviations. Write 'Mon', not 'Mon.' (unless the abbreviation ends a sentence). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases/abbreviations)
- **Full stop in common abbreviation** (`abbrev-common-full-stop`): Don't put a full stop after abbreviations (unless the abbreviation ends a sentence). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases/abbreviations)
- **Full stop after unit symbol** (`abbrev-unit-full-stop`): Don't put a full stop after unit symbols. Write 'kg', not 'kg.' (unless the symbol ends a sentence). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/measurement-and-units)
- **Pluralised unit symbol** (`abbrev-unit-plural`): Don't add 's' to unit symbols. Write '5 kg', not '5 kgs'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/measurement-and-units)
- **Apostrophe in abbreviation plural** (`abbrev-plural-apostrophe`): Don't use an apostrophe to pluralise abbreviations. Write 'DVDs', not 'DVD's'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/shortened-words-and-phrases/abbreviations)

## Government terms

- **Commonwealth government** (`govt-commonwealth-government`): Use 'Australian Government', not 'Commonwealth government', to refer to the national government of Australia. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/names-and-terms/government-terms)
- **Minister of (wrong preposition)** (`govt-minister-preposition`): Use 'Minister for', not 'Minister of', when referring to a minister's portfolio. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/names-and-terms/government-terms)
- **Secretary for (wrong preposition)** (`govt-secretary-preposition`): Use 'Secretary of', not 'Secretary for', when referring to a departmental secretary. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/names-and-terms/government-terms)
- **Generic department reference** (`govt-generic-department`): Use lower case for generic references to departments. Write 'the department', not 'the Department'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/names-and-terms/government-terms)
- **Generic minister reference** (`govt-generic-minister`): Use lower case for generic references to ministers. Write 'the minister', not 'the Minister'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/names-and-terms/government-terms)
- **Generic government body reference** (`govt-generic-agency`): Use lower case for generic references to government bodies (for example, 'the agency', 'the board', 'the commission'). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/names-and-terms/government-terms)

## Readability

- **Watch words** (`watch-words`): Consider replacing this word or phrase with a plain language alternative. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/clear-language-and-writing-style/plain-language-and-word-choice)
- **Long sentence** (`readability-sentence-length`): Sentences over 25 words can be harder to read. Consider breaking up this long sentence. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/accessible-and-inclusive-content/how-people-read)
- **Wordy phrase** (`wordy-phrases`): Consider replacing this wordy phrase with a plain language alternative. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/clear-language-and-writing-style/plain-language-and-word-choice)
- **Double negative** (`readability-double-negative`): Double negatives can lead to misunderstandings, so avoid them. Write 'common' instead of 'not uncommon'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/clear-language-and-writing-style/sentences)
- **'if' and 'unless' in one sentence** (`readability-if-unless`): Don't use 'if' and 'unless' in the same sentence - the combined conditions are hard to follow. Split them into 2 sentences. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/clear-language-and-writing-style/sentences)
- **Passive voice with named doer** (`readability-passive-voice`): Prefer active voice. When the doer appears after 'by', the sentence can usually be turned around: 'the board approved the report', not 'the report was approved by the board'. Passive voice is sometimes appropriate - this is advisory only. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/clear-language-and-writing-style/sentences)

## Numbers and measurements

- **Ordinal numeral instead of word** (`numbers-ordinal-words`): Use words for ordinals up to 'ninth'. Use numerals for 10th and above. Exceptions: centuries ('1st century'), reference editions ('2nd edn') and organisation names. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/ordinal-numbers)
- **Superscript ordinal** (`punct-superscript-ordinal`): Don't write ordinal suffixes in superscript. Superscript may not be accessible to people who use screen readers. Use words for ordinals up to 'ninth' and plain text numerals for 10th and above. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/ordinal-numbers)
- **Use words for zero and one** (`numbers-zero-one`): Write 'zero' and 'one' as words, not numerals. The numerals '0' and '1' can be hard to distinguish from letters in some fonts. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/choosing-numerals-or-words)
- **Choosing numerals or words** (`numbers-words-to-numerals`): Use numerals for numbers 2 and above in text. Write '3 options', not 'three options'. Exceptions include the start of sentences, fractions and figures of speech. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/choosing-numerals-or-words)
- **Percent with numeral** (`numbers-percent-spelling`): Use the percentage sign (%) with numerals. Write '85%', not '85 percent' or '85 per cent'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/percentages)
- **Space before percentage sign** (`numbers-percent-space`): Don't use a space between a number and the percentage sign. Write '15%', not '15 %'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/percentages)
- **Missing leading zero** (`numbers-leading-zero`): Decimal values less than one should have a '0' before the decimal point. Write '0.5', not '.5'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/fractions-and-decimals)
- **Numeral at start of sentence** (`numbers-start-sentence`): Don't start a sentence with a numeral. Write the number in words, or rephrase the sentence. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/choosing-numerals-or-words)
- **Missing comma in large number** (`numbers-comma-thousands`): Use commas in numbers with 4 or more digits. Write '2,500', not '2500'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/choosing-numerals-or-words)
- **Word number with unit symbol** (`numbers-measurement-words`): Always use numerals with units of measurement. Write '5 km', not 'five km'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/measurement-and-units)
- **Imperial units** (`numbers-imperial-units`): Australia uses the metric system. Consider using metric units unless you have a specific reason for imperial (for example, historical quotes, US audience). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/measurement-and-units)
- **Space after currency symbol** (`numbers-currency-space`): Don't put a space between the currency symbol and the numerals. Write '$50', not '$ 50'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/currency)
- **'per cent' used as a noun** (`numbers-percent-noun`): Use 'percentage' as the noun. Write 'the percentage of Australians', not 'the per cent of Australians'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/percentages)
- **Programming comparison operators** (`numbers-comparison-operators`): '>=' and '<=' are not mathematical notation. Use the proper symbols (≥, ≤) or words ('greater than or equal to', 'less than or equal to'). [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/mathematical-relationships)
- **Missing space between number and unit** (`numbers-unit-space`): Put a non-breaking space between the number and the unit of measurement. Write '5 kg', not '5kg'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/measurement-and-units)
- **Telephone number format** (`numbers-phone-format`): Write Australian phone numbers in standard chunks with non-breaking spaces: '02 6244 1000' (landline), '0491 570 159' (mobile), '1300 975 707', '13 24 68'. Don't use parentheses, hyphens or unbroken digits. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/telephone-numbers)

## Lists

- **Semicolon at end of list item** (`list-semicolon`): Don't use semicolons at the end of list items. Use minimal punctuation in lists. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/lists)
- **Comma at end of list item** (`list-trailing-comma`): Don't use commas at the end of list items. Use minimal punctuation in lists. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/lists)
- **'And' or 'or' at end of list item** (`list-and-or`): Don't use 'and' or 'or' at the end of list items. The bullet or number structure makes these unnecessary. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/lists)
- **'Etc.' in list** (`list-etc`): Don't write 'etc.' at the end of a list to show the list is incomplete. Use a lead-in like 'including' or 'for example' instead. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/lists)
- **List items not parallel** (`list-parallel-structure`): Every item in a list should follow the same grammatical pattern so they all read smoothly after the lead-in. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/lists)
- **List item capitalisation** (`list-item-capitals`): Sentence and stand-alone list items start with a capital letter. Fragment list items start lower case unless they begin with a proper noun. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/lists)
- **List item end punctuation** (`list-item-end-punctuation`): Sentence list items end with a full stop. In fragment lists, only the last item ends with a full stop. Stand-alone list items have no end punctuation. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/lists)

## Inclusive language

- **'ATSI'** (`inclusive-atsi`): Never use the shorthand 'ATSI'. Write 'Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander' in full, or 'First Nations'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/accessible-and-inclusive-content/inclusive-language/aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islander-peoples)
- **'Aborigines' or 'Aboriginals' as nouns** (`inclusive-aborigines`): These nouns are outdated and can cause offence. Write 'Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people' or 'First Nations people'. ('Aboriginal' as an adjective is fine.) [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/accessible-and-inclusive-content/inclusive-language/aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islander-peoples)
- **Lower-case 'indigenous' or 'aboriginal' for peoples** (`inclusive-indigenous-capital`): Capitalise 'Indigenous' and 'Aboriginal' when referring to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people. Lower case is only correct for generic uses such as 'indigenous species'. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/accessible-and-inclusive-content/inclusive-language/aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islander-peoples)
- **'Christian name'** (`inclusive-christian-name`): Not everyone has a Christian name. Write 'given name' instead. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/accessible-and-inclusive-content/inclusive-language/cultural-and-linguistic-diversity)

## Accessibility

- **Underlined text that is not a link** (`format-underline-not-link`): Underlining signals hyperlinks. Don't underline text for emphasis or headings - users will try to click it. Use bold or a heading style instead. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/headings)
- **Non-breaking space in times, units and phone numbers** (`accessibility-nonbreaking-space`): Use a non-breaking space so the number and what follows it stay together on one line - between numerals and 'am'/'pm', between numbers and units, and between phone number chunks. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/numbers-and-measurements/dates-and-time)

## Links

- **Generic link text** (`link-generic-text`): Link text should describe the destination. Generic text like 'click here' or 'read more' doesn't make sense out of context, especially for screen reader users who navigate by links. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/structuring-content/links)
- **Full stop inside link text** (`link-full-stop`): Don't include the sentence's full stop in the link text. Move it outside the link. [Guidance](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/grammar-punctuation-and-conventions/punctuation/full-stops)
