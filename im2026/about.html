Most AI tools ask you to trust the model. This one doesn't.

Proof Positive never shows you AI output that its deterministic rule engine has not checked first. The engine codifies more than 100 rules from the Australian Government Style Manual; generative AI is used only where fixing something takes judgement – and then the rules mark the AI's homework.

How the draft-and-verify loop works

The rules check your text

A deterministic rule engine runs in your browser: the same input gives the same answer every time, and nothing is uploaded.

AI drafts where judgement is needed

Rewording a tangled sentence or making list items parallel has no mechanical fix – so Claude drafts a suggestion.

The rules mark the AI's homework

Every AI draft goes back through the rule engine before you see it. Mechanical breaches are corrected automatically; anything left is flagged, never hidden.

You stay the editor

Suggestions are always labelled ✦, always reviewable, and never applied to your document without you.

The Ask tool adds one more safeguard: answers are grounded in extracts of the actual Style Manual pages retrieved for your question, and can only cite pages the bot really read – it cannot invent rules or references.

Bot card

Name
Proof Positive (IM2026) – an entry in the APS Innovation Month 2026 ‘Build a Bureaucrat Bot’ challenge.
Purpose
Help people who write for government apply the Australian Government Style Manual: check Word documents and pasted text, rewrite dense passages into plain English, format lists, build author–date citations and answer style questions.
Intended users
Australian public servants and anyone writing to Style Manual conventions. It is a demonstration and a drafting aid – not an official government service and not a substitute for editorial judgement.
Information used
Public Style Manual content only, © Commonwealth of Australia, used under CC BY 4.0. The Ask tool retrieves from 146 saved Style Manual pages. There are no user accounts and no database: documents are processed in your browser and never uploaded, and text sent to AI features is not stored by the tool or used to train models.
Tools used
  • A hand-built rule engine (plain JavaScript, no framework) codifying more than 100 Style Manual rules – it runs entirely in the browser.
  • Anthropic's Claude models via a rate-limited Cloudflare Worker proxy, so the API key never reaches the browser. AI features are marked ✦.
  • GitHub Pages for hosting. No analytics, no cookies, no trackers.
Limitations
  • The rule engine covers a codified subset of the Style Manual, not every nuance of the full guidance.
  • AI suggestions can misread meaning or tone – always compare them with your original. The verification step catches mechanical breaches, not factual or semantic errors.
  • Pasted-text checks infer headings and lists heuristically; the Word document check reads real formatting and is more accurate.
  • AI features have daily usage limits to keep the free tool affordable.
Risks and mitigations
  • Invented rules or links – Ask answers only from retrieved Style Manual extracts and can only cite pages it actually read.
  • Wrong 'corrections' – quoted examples and URLs are masked so the engine never rewrites them; every automatic fix is deterministic and reported.
  • Sensitive information – every AI entry point carries the same warning: don't enter classified, sensitive or personal information.
  • Cost abuse – per-user and whole-of-tool daily request limits, enforced server-side.
Provenance
Built by Jen Robertson as a personal project for Innovation Month 2026. The rule engine grew from Style Manual Check, a Word add-in developed for working-group testing.

Accessibility

A style tool that checks accessibility guidance should hold itself to the same standard. Proof Positive is built to meet WCAG 2.1 AA: semantic HTML with a skip link on every page, full keyboard operation, visible focus indicators, status messages announced to screen readers, no information conveyed by colour alone, and layouts that reflow at 400% zoom.

If you hit a barrier, please email listformatterfeedback@gmail.com – reports are genuinely welcome and fixes are usually quick.

This bot is an entry in the ‘Build a Bureaucrat Bot’ challenge run as part of APS Innovation Month 2026 (IM2026). In the spirit of the challenge – and of Australia's AI Ethics Principles – it tries to show one repeatable pattern: let generative AI draft, and let deterministic rules verify. Any team could apply the same pattern to their own guidance, legislation or business rules.

About this bot

Proof Positive (IM2026) is an entry in the APS Innovation Month 2026 ‘Build a Bureaucrat Bot’ challenge. It is a personal project, not an official government service, and it does not replace editorial judgement or the Australian Government Style Manual.

Your data

Rule-based checks run entirely in your browser – nothing is uploaded. Features marked with ✦ send the text you choose to the Claude API (Anthropic) to generate a suggestion; it is not used to train models. Don't enter classified, sensitive or personal information.
